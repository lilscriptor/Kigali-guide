Moving to Kigali to study: what first-year students should know

Written in Kigali by David Mazimpaka. Prices last checked: November 2026.

Moving to a new city to study is exciting, and it comes with a lot of small decisions: where to live, how to pay for things, how to get to class. This page covers what new students and their parents usually ask first. The prices are typical amounts in Rwandan francs (RWF) and they change, so always confirm before you pay.

Quick facts
  • Cheapest areas to rent: Nyamirambo, Remera, Kimironko, Gisozi and Kicukiro.
  • Phone: you need a local SIM and your passport.
  • Everyday payments: mobile money (MoMo) and small cash notes.
  • Getting around: city bus, moto-taxi and ride apps.

Before you arrive

  • Confirm your admission, your fees and any student entry requirements directly with your university, and check the official Rwanda immigration website for current rules. Do not rely on a friend or an agent for this.
  • Ask your university whether it offers housing, and how far your campus is from the areas you like.
  • Buy travel and health insurance, and check any vaccine requirements with your doctor.
  • Check your own government's travel advice for Rwanda the week you travel.
  • Bring your passport, admission letter and copies of your documents.

Where to live

Many students rent a room or a small studio near campus or near a bus route. Rent depends on the area, whether it is furnished, and what is included. Here are four areas that students often consider:

  • Nyamirambo: lively and local, and one of the cheapest areas. A room can cost roughly 150,000 to 300,000 RWF per month.
  • Remera and Kimironko: busy, with markets and many buses. Studios are often around 350,000 to 600,000 RWF per month.
  • Gisozi: newer buildings and a good choice for single students.
  • Kicukiro and Kagarama: calmer, near schools, popular with families.

Prices vary by building and landlord, and they are higher for furnished places and for newer buildings.

Rules for renting safely

  • Never pay before you see the house and have a signed contract.
  • Ask what the deposit is, and get a receipt for every payment. A deposit of 2 to 3 months of rent is common.
  • Make sure the contract is in RWF.
  • Check the water, the Cashpower (electricity) meter, WiFi and security.
  • Visit the place at night as well as during the day.

Phone and data

Get a local SIM from MTN or Airtel, and bring your passport because registration is required. An eSIM that only gives data will not open a MoMo wallet, so you need a local SIM and number for that. Data bundles are cheap. For example, MTN bundles valid for 30 days have been 3 GB for 3,000 RWF, 7 GB for 5,000 RWF and 30 GB for 10,000 RWF, and an Airtel bundle of 3 GB for 30 days has cost around 3,500 RWF.

Money

  • Open a MoMo wallet at an MTN or Airtel agent. It is free, and many shops, restaurants and drivers accept it.
  • ATMs have per-withdrawal limits, so plan your withdrawals.
  • Keep small notes, like 500, 1,000 and 2,000 RWF, for motos and markets.
  • Never share your PIN or an OTP code with anyone.

Getting to campus

  • City bus: the cheapest option, often 200 to 600 RWF. You need a Tap&Go card, because you cannot pay cash on board.
  • Moto-taxi: a short trip in town is often 500 to 1,000 RWF. Always wear the helmet and agree the price first.
  • Ride apps: Yego and Move give you a fixed price. There is no Uber in Rwanda.

Eating on a student budget

Local restaurants and lunch buffets are the cheapest way to eat well. A simple meal of pilau, beans, fried banana and spinach can cost about 3,000 to 5,000 RWF. Many places deliver, and ordering directly by WhatsApp is often cheaper than using an app, so ask about the delivery fee first.

A note for parents

Kigali is generally calm and organised, and many families find it comfortable for students. Government travel advice for Rwanda has become more cautious in 2026, mainly because of the DR Congo border region. Read the current advice for your country, and ask your student's university about its safety arrangements.

Questions

How much should I budget each month?

It depends mainly on rent and how you eat. Rent is usually the biggest cost, followed by food and transport. Ask current students at your university what they spend, and add a buffer for the first month.

Do I need a Rwandan bank account?

Many students use MoMo for daily payments. Ask your university about its requirements for fees and any bank accounts.

Is it safe to rent through an agent?

Be careful. See the place yourself, get a signed contract in RWF, and never send money before you have done both.

Can I arrive before the semester starts?

Yes, and it helps. Arriving a week early gives you time to find a place, get a SIM and MoMo, and learn the bus routes.
