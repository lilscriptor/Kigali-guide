Is Kigali safe for first-time visitors?

Written in Kigali by David Mazimpaka. Last checked: November 2026.

Short answer: day to day, Kigali is calm and orderly, and tourists are rarely the target of crime. But official travel advice for Rwanda has become more cautious in 2026, mostly because of the conflict in eastern DR Congo. Read what your own government says before you book.

Quick facts
  • Kigali itself: generally calm. Petty theft happens, and violent crime against foreigners is rare.
  • Main caution: areas close to the border with DR Congo.
  • Volcanoes National Park: not part of the US advisory's area of highest concern.
  • Biggest everyday risks: road accidents and opportunistic theft.

What governments say right now

Advice changes, and countries do not agree with each other, so check yours the week you travel. As of November 2026:

  • United States: an advisory issued in June 2026 tells travelers to reconsider travel to Rwanda, citing crime, unrest and health risks. It says not to travel within 10 km of the border with DR Congo, and it says Volcanoes National Park is not in that area.
  • United Kingdom: advises against all but essential travel to parts of Rwanda, mainly the border regions.
  • Canada: notes that tourists are usually not targeted, and advises being aware of your surroundings and avoiding isolated areas and travel outside major cities after dark.
  • Sweden: describes Rwanda as one of Africa's safest countries, while noting that some crime occurs in Kigali.

Read the full, current advice here: US State Department, UK FCDO, and Government of Canada.

Where to be extra careful

The caution is mostly about the western border with DR Congo, which includes areas around Lake Kivu. If you plan to visit Lake Kivu, check your government's current advice for the exact town and ask your hotel or a licensed operator about the situation before you go. Kigali and the Volcanoes National Park area in the north are in a different part of the country from the western border.

Everyday safety tips for Kigali

  • Keep your phone, camera and wallet out of sight in crowds and markets.
  • Avoid isolated areas, and avoid walking alone at night.
  • After dark, use a ride app or a driver you trust instead of flagging transport on the street.
  • Arrange your airport pickup through your hotel or a ride app.
  • If someone demands your valuables, hand them over. Your safety matters more than the phone.
  • Do not photograph soldiers, police, government buildings or the airport.

Road safety

Traffic accidents are one of the biggest real risks for visitors. Moto-taxis are convenient, but always wear the helmet, agree the price first, and avoid riding at night if you can.

Health and insurance

  • Buy travel insurance that covers medical care and evacuation. Ambulance services can be limited.
  • Tap water is not considered safe to drink, so use bottled or filtered water.
  • Check current vaccine and health requirements with your doctor before you travel.

Questions

Is it safe to walk around Kigali during the day?

Most visitors find the city calm in the daytime. Use normal city sense, keep valuables out of sight, and be aware of your surroundings.

Are moto-taxis safe?

Road accidents are a real risk. Wear the helmet, avoid night rides, and choose a ride app so the driver and price are recorded.

Do I need travel insurance?

Yes. Choose a policy that covers medical care and evacuation, because emergency services can be limited.

Should I cancel my trip because of the advisories?

That is your decision. Read your government's current advice, check the specific places you plan to visit, and talk to your insurer and your hotel or tour operator.
